ARC-Lean: when does scientific evidence actually identify the claim we want to make?

A scientific result can reproduce, predict, or correlate—and still leave several mechanisms compatible with the measurements. ARC-Lean separates empirical decision, identifiability, and the strongest conclusion the evidence is licensed to support.

01 / THE PROBLEM

“The result is solid” hides three different questions.

Most scientific evaluation collapses evidence retrieval, empirical fit, causal identification, and claim wording into one judgment. ARC-Lean makes each obligation explicit.

Decision: is the claim supported, falsified, or inconclusive in the declared scope? Identifiability: do all admissible models that match the measurements agree on the target? Promotion ceiling: what is the strongest assertion the evidence permits without silently adding assumptions?

05 / WHAT LEAN PROVES

Trusted deduction inside a declared model class—not empirical truth

Lean checks that the formal conclusion follows from encoded assumptions. It cannot certify that the assumptions describe the material, that evidence extraction is correct, or that the admissible model class is complete.

Conditional non-identifiability receipt
theorem sameObservation_differentTarget
    (hobs : observe w₁ = observe w₂)
    (htarget : target w₁ ≠ target w₂) :
    ¬ Identifies observe target := by
  intro h
  exact htarget (h w₁ w₂ hobs)
The receipt establishes
  • the counterexample is logically sufficient under the formal definitions;
  • assumption use and theorem dependencies are machine-checkable;
  • the conclusion cannot silently exceed the declared proposition.
It does not establish
  • that either world is physically realized;
  • that the scientific model class contains every plausible mechanism;
  • that the paper's measurement was extracted without error.
